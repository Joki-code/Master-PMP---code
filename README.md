# Master-PMP---code
Public repository containing the code for my master's thesis.

Background and Objective: Fossil-fuel-fired heating systems still account for 52% of Swiss boilers in 2024. This study analyzes the effectiveness of subsidy programs 
implemented by municipalities in the canton of Vaud to accelerate their replacement—a level of government that has received little attention to date.

Methodology: The study uses data from the Buildings and Housing Statistics (FSO) for the period 2021–2024. It employs a discrete-time log-log (Cloglog) model based on
103,012 buildings to determine whether subsidies have an incentive effect, while controlling for building, owner, and municipality characteristics.

Key Findings:

Overall Effect: The effect of the subsidies is statistically marginal, with a hazard ratio of 1.06. Overall, the subsidy does not appear to serve as an incentive,
resulting in a strong free-rider effect. The average cost per metric ton of CO₂ avoided is very low (4 CHF), while the marginal cost (269 CHF) is much higher,
suggesting that 94% of beneficiaries would have taken action even without the subsidy.

Spatial Heterogeneity: The program’s impact varies widely. A Conditional Average Treatment Effect (CATE) analysis reveals that:

        In urban areas, the subsidy is ineffective.
        In intermediate-sized municipalities, the effect is positive (+9.9%).
        In rural municipalities, the effect is highly significant (+23.3%).

Underlying Mechanism: The hypothesis is that the program’s effectiveness in rural and intermediate areas does not stem from the amount of the subsidies, but rather from non-financial tools (advice, word of mouth, group purchasing) facilitated by a denser social network, which amplify the effect of the financial incentive.

Conclusion: The Vaud municipal grant program is considered ineffective due to a strong free-rider effect. Its apparent positive impact is a statistical illusion that masks a mixed reality: it works only in rural municipalities—likely due to social factors—and fails in urban areas.
